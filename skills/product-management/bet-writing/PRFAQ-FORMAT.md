# PR/FAQ format

Amazon-style working-backwards document. Create when the format is chosen. The press release is written first and rewritten most: if it cannot be written, the FAQs are answering the wrong questions. Total length: one page of press release, then as many FAQ entries as have real answers. An FAQ entry with a hand-wavy answer is an open question; mark it so in place.

```markdown
# <Product or feature name>: <one-line headline a buyer would understand>

## Press release

**<City>, <date>.** <Company> today announced <name>, <one sentence on what it does for whom>.

<Paragraph: the problem, in the customer's words. What they do today and why it fails.>

<Paragraph: how life changes, from the customer's point of view. What is now true that wasn't. No mechanisms.>

<Quote from a company leader on why this matters.>

<Paragraph: how a customer gets started. What they do, not what we do.>

<Quote from a customer, plausible and specific. Say if invented.>

<One line: availability.>

## External FAQ
Questions someone in the market would ask. At minimum:
- Who is this for, and who is it not for?
- I don't use you today. Why would this make me start?
- What do I have to do differently?
- What happens to <the thing I do today>?
- What does it cost, and what changes on my bill?

## Internal FAQ
Questions our own people would ask. At minimum:
- What are we not doing, and why?
- What do we believe that, if wrong, sinks this?
- What are we leaving for engineering to decide?
- What does success look like, and for which customer?
```

## Voice of the press release

The press release is written for someone in the market reading it cold, who may never have used the product. Set aside the author's usual register for this section only.

- Assume no knowledge of what exists today. An improvement to an existing capability is written as what the product now does for them, never as a change to something they've never seen.
- Present tense, third person, the plain words a buyer would use. Lead with what is now true for the customer, then how they get there.
- Two quotes, each specific enough that it could not be said about another product.
- Mechanisms and internal vocabulary belong in the internal FAQ. If the release needs our jargon to make sense, the problem isn't understood yet.
- Confident and warm. Superlatives and "excited to announce" are out.

The test: could a prospect at a company that has never bought from us read this and know whether it is for them?
